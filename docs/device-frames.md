# Model-specific independent preview frames

The **Projection settings → Show phone frame (global)** switch (also available under the independent window's **Window → Window style**) is off by default and saved globally across restarts. It immediately updates all supported independent windows and applies to future windows, including devices whose model metadata arrives later. The switch remains available without a selected device; unknown models and media playback windows keep their existing presentation. All settings entry points reflect the same saved preference. Existing per-device settings migrate to enabled if any device previously enabled frames; an explicit global choice takes precedence afterward. Frames disappear in fullscreen/maximized mode. Changing the option never opens a window, starts capture, or modifies captured video or recordings. Body-color choices remain per device, and a failed apply or save restores the previous preference and window states.

Both main and floating Projection settings expose the same body-color choices. A failed save restores the prior choice and shows feedback; selecting Automatic clears the override. Open descriptions and options refresh when the language changes. Disabling a frame drops its native CPU artwork and GPU textures even when capture has cleared its last image; enabling supplies the chosen artwork again.

## Coverage

Model names now use the same complete offline display catalog as panel negotiation: **101 products / 174 ProductTypes**, cross-checked against the current IPSW index on 2026-11-13. This fixes the former separate name table that stopped at iPhone 17 and omitted all iPads. In particular, iPhone19,2 resolves to iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone19,3 / iPhone19,7 resolve to iPhone 18 Pro Max.

Frame artwork includes **101 of 101 products / 174 of 174 ProductTypes**, grouped into **94 device profiles plus Duo's additional inner-display profile, with 311 finish assets**. See [the complete coverage table](device-frame-coverage.md) for each model's finishes and artwork type. This count includes original SVG drawings for 19 products (20 profiles, separating GSM/CDMA iPhone 4). Some imported models still have only a subset of finishes. Unknown future identifiers remain disabled; they never inherit a similar-resolution device. Model coverage does not imply that original Apple artwork or every automatic color code is available.

The remaining models are drawn specifically for their declared ProductTypes in `config/device-frame-drawings.json`: original iPhone, 3G, 3GS, 4, 4s, SE 3, 16e, 17e; original iPad, iPad 2/3/4/5/6, mini 1/2/3, Air 1 and Pro 9.7. Their 45 editable SVGs are in `src/App/Assets/DeviceFrameDrawings/`, rendered by `scripts/draw_device_frames.py`. Official body dimensions, panel pixels and density constrain the layout. Sensor/button/notch positions, corner radii and finish shading are photo-based visual estimates, not physically measured manufacturing drawings. Legacy panels are rendered at 2x density to preserve line detail. Early devices have no invented front camera; square Home icons and Touch ID rings are separate. 16e/17e have a connected notch rather than an island. Settings explicitly identify these as custom-drawn frames, including after finish changes. Their color lists contain names only; no unverified numeric housing codes are added.

iPad (10th generation) has its own Wi-Fi/cellular profile and four named finishes (blue, pink, silver, yellow). It reuses Apple's A16 front-view PNGs after review of both official specifications and the iPad 10 dimensional drawing: 248.6 × 179.5 × 7 mm body, 1640 × 2360 display, landscape front camera, top Touch ID and volume-button placement. The association is explicit in `config/device-frame-shared-fronts.json`, rather than inferred from panel resolution. This is reviewed front-view reuse, not a dedicated iPad 10 bezel export or physical-device measurement. It does not import the A16 profile's numeric housing codes; automatic detection accepts only the iPad 10 profile's own reviewed tokens.

iPhone 5, iPhone 6, iPhone 6 Plus and the first-generation 12.9-inch iPad Pro use the model-specific vector renderings credited by `pixelsign/html5-device-mockups`. They are not Apple bezel exports or physical-device measurements. Only their publisher-specified rectangular screen is cleared; original PNG hashes, derived PNG hashes, coordinates and credits are recorded in `config/device-frame-supplements.json` and the generated frame catalog. The first-generation iPad Pro association is supported by its April 2017 asset commit, before the second generation's release.

Apple exports retain their original bezel, hardware cutout, buttons and sensor details. Every finish records its own canvas and screen rectangle: offsets can differ even within one model. Center-connected transparency determines the screen mask; all artwork hashes and panel aspect ratios are checked. The frame depicts the hardware cutout; live Dynamic Island animations remain part of the mirrored picture.

Duo has separate unmodified Apple inner-open and outer-closed portrait PNGs for Night Sky and Star White. Its captured frame's orientation-independent aspect ratio selects only between those two panels of the same ProductType; downscaled and landscape frames use the same rule. Panel changes preserve the manual finish and update artwork, window aspect and hit testing together. Without a captured frame it defaults to the outer panel. iPad Pro M5 now uses its own Apple archive rather than a shared M4 profile.

## Automatic enclosure color

Device enumeration stores the initial USB housing metadata and ProductType in `device-appearance.json`, keyed by normalized device identity. Reads with no housing fields retain the recorded housing color. A fresh housing read replaces the pair together so a stale second field cannot conflict with a new color. The cache survives restart, excludes wireless identities, validates the model before reusing codes, and is separate from manual color overrides. Rendering reads the recorded values without querying USB. An initial successful housing read is still required; caching cannot infer data the device has never supplied.

Missing housing fields are also requested individually on the existing metadata tunnel. Sparse refreshes retain previously read colors. Active capture sessions still avoid probing USB metadata.

The main Settings → Projection page offers a per-device body-color selector, persisted by device identity. Manual choices use original model artwork, survive restart, and are explicitly labeled as manual. Selecting Automatic removes the override. This allows correct artwork when capture mode or wireless transport cannot expose housing metadata.

The existing USB Lockdown metadata query reads `DeviceEnclosureColor` and `DeviceHousingColor` as strings or nonnegative integers. A separate cached native API exposes these fields without changing the existing device-info ABI or issuing queries during rendering. `DeviceColor` is retained for diagnostics but never used to select the housing: it describes front glass. For AirPlay, automatic selection can reuse cached housing metadata from the wired device in the same binding profile when both entries have the exact same ProductType.

Independent windows apply known model and finish information before their initial show. Open background windows also re-evaluate appearance after inventory reconciliation or a committed binding change, even while video dimensions remain constant. The renderer preserves confirmed wireless landscape orientation through hidden sensor pixels and waits for two distinct frames with matching evidence when entering landscape. A same-size 180° flip can change the selected side after three consistent observations spanning at least 100 ms. This depends on the new side being visible in the mirrored frames.

- Known names and enclosure-code tokens are matched within the exact ProductType. Reviewed tokens live in `config/apple-device-frame-colors.json`; importing artwork does not invent new numeric meanings.
- Missing, unknown or conflicting colors use that profile's declared default finish, with an explicit “color not confirmed” description. The displayed fallback name always matches the actual artwork, including silver, blue and metallic defaults.
- Exact spelling aliases are supported. Hex colors are not approximated to nearby finishes. Color encodings vary between OS and hardware generations, so the presence of an artwork finish does not guarantee automatic matching of every device-returned token.

Numeric mappings were cross-checked against Apple's device icons at `https://statici.icloud.com/fmipmobile/deviceImages-9.0/iPhone/{ProductType}-1-{code}-0/online-infobox__3x.png`. For 12 mini, codes 1/2/6/9/12 correspond to black/white/red/blue/green. For 17, codes 1/2/9/17/18 correspond to black/white/mist blue/lavender/sage. On 2026-11-13, the user's cached iPhone18,3 metadata contained front color 1, enclosure color 2 and housing color 2; the owner confirmed that phone is white. This real-device observation resolves the earlier CDN ambiguity (its code 2 icon returned identical bytes to code 17). Other color mappings still require real-device validation. Apple may change metadata encoding between devices and OS versions; see the [libimobiledevice discussion](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/libimobiledevice/issues/1391).

The initial implementation used synthetic metadata. Subsequent real-device cache inspection confirmed both iPhone13,1 and iPhone18,3 return enclosure/housing code 2 for the owner-confirmed white phones, with no manual color override. Regression tests cover those values and automatic selection.

For iPhone 18 Pro / Pro Max, Apple's per-ProductType Find My icons were checked for all three identifiers (iPhone19,2 / 19,3 / 19,7): 1 black, 2 silver, 6 burgundy, 9 glacier. The two Pro Max identifiers return identical artwork for each corresponding token. Exact URLs and hashes are recorded in `config/apple-device-frame-colors.json`. These model-scoped mappings are covered by regression tests; a connected iPhone 18's actual Lockdown values have not been physically validated in this session.

## Sources and reproduction

The main artwork source is the versioned [fastlane/frameit-frames 1772014847](https://github.com/fastlane/frameit-frames/tree/gh-pages/1772014847) collection. The collection uses Apple's product bezels; [Apple Design Resources](https://developer.apple.com/design/resources/#product-bezels) publishes the device art and its applicable usage guidance. Artwork is Apple material and is not relicensed under the application's source-code license. `config/apple-device-frames.json` records exact source URLs and SHA-256 digests. Runtime operation is offline. Supplementary source PNGs come from Apple's `Bezel-iPhone-15.dmg`, `Bezel-iPhone-18.dmg`, `Bezel-iPhone-Duo.dmg`, `Bezel-iPad-(A16).dmg`, `Bezel-iPad-Air.dmg` (March 2022), `Bezel-iPad-Air-(M4).dmg`, `Bezel-iPad-mini-(A20-Pro).dmg`, `Bezel-iPad-Pro-(M5).dmg`, and pinned snapshots of `jonnyjackson26/device-frames-media`, `TimBroddin/appleframer.com` and `pixelsign/html5-device-mockups`. All source links and individual SHA-256 hashes are recorded in the catalog; the source indexes are checked in at `config/device-frame-source-index.json` and `config/device-frame-supplements.json`. Delivery notices, licenses and graphical-author credits are included under `licenses/device-frames/`.

`python scripts/import_device_frames.py` imports the pinned source catalog. Direct Apple disk images must first be extracted into `work/phone-frame-research/apple-dmg/{iphone-15,iphone-18,iphone-duo,ipad-a16,ipad-air,ipad-air-m4,ipad-mini-a20,ipad-pro-m5}`. The importer measures exact transparency, checks the panel aspect, applies the separately reviewed token table, and rejects identifier/asset-name collisions. The supplement manifest supplies the older vector assets' pinned blob URLs and published screen rectangles. Missing source packages must be obtained from the exact source URLs in the existing catalog. It does not substitute a different body to increase a coverage counter.

The importer also renders the original SVG drawings using `resvg-py==0.5.0`. Either `python scripts/import_device_frames.py --drawings-only` or `python scripts/draw_device_frames.py` refreshes those drawings together with their catalog hashes, geometry and coverage while preserving imported artwork; unchanged SVG/PNG pairs are reused only after both hashes match. The renderer needs no network or source photos. Original drawings and generated assets use the project's source-code license; third-party artwork retains its own license.

Run `python scripts/prepare_device_frames.py` to verify PNG hashes, canvas dimensions, default-finish metadata, measured screen rectangles and GPU size limits, regenerate screen masks, and generate the raw GPU review fixtures. `--check-only` validates without writing files. Pillow, numpy and scipy are required. By default 39 native fixtures (the existing 19 plus every newly drawn profile) are written; `--fixtures` emits every finish and requires considerably more disk space. The mask is the transparent screen region connected to the center, excluding transparent exterior pixels. Partial alpha at the original screen boundary is preserved, and notch/island pixels are excluded from touch hit testing.

GPU rendering uses the original premultiplied artwork plus a separate screen mask. Screen placement, aspect ratio, pointer mapping, and key-mapping overlays all use the same model's screen rectangle. The artwork rotates with the source's portrait/landscape layout and the window rotation command. A landscape source without direction metadata uses the clockwise landscape hardware orientation.

## Validation

- Run `iPhoneMirror.Core.Tests.exe --device-appearance-only` for native color token parsing, cached metadata preservation, and appearance API contracts.
- `python -m unittest discover -s tests -p device_frames_test.py` checks rejected coordinate mismatches, opaque/open screens and preserved partial alpha.

- `dotnet build src/App.Runtime.Tests -c Release`
- Run the built runtime test executable with `--phone-frame work/phone-frame-review` for color/model matching, metadata preservation, fallback behavior, screen/pointer geometry in four rotations, mask hit testing, and switch rollback.
- Run it with `--settings-categories work/phone-frame-settings-review` for actual main/floating color synchronization, persisted manual/automatic selection, failed-save rollback, live language refresh and the five-language/two-theme settings review.
- Set `IPHONE_MIRROR_FRAME_FIXTURES` to the absolute `work/phone-frame-review` directory, then run `iPhoneMirror.Core.Tests.exe --preview-static-frame-only` for real GPU rendering of 39 device/display profiles (Home button, notch, island, iPad and Duo's two panels), direct/limited passes, cutout pixels, four window rotations, native landscape sources, fullscreen restoration, paused-image redraw and artwork release with paused/empty sources. The test writes portrait/landscape review screenshots of synthetic content; it does not access a phone.

Physical-device comparison is still required before claiming pixel-for-pixel equivalence to a particular phone's live orientation, color and display mode.

## Review findings addressed

- X and XR special-character names can collide after ASCII slug generation; the importer now normalizes XR explicitly and rejects duplicate asset IDs.
- Different finish assets can have different pixel offsets/canvases. Each now carries its own geometry and the window recalculates dimensions after a finish change.
- Fallback artwork is not always black. The profile declares its default color and the UI reports that exact finish.
- Expanded artwork is weakly cached after decoding. Enabled preview windows and mapping overlays strongly retain their own hit-test mask, so GC cannot move PNG decoding into pointer handlers. Disabling/changing the frame or closing its window releases that reference; inactive models remain collectible.
- Descriptions carry the actual ProductType separately from shared artwork and use the shared model-name catalog. Shared iPad 7/8/9, Air M2/M3 and Pro M4/M5 geometry no longer causes the UI to report the artwork's oldest model name.
- Fresh partial housing reads no longer merge with stale second-field values. Invalid cached null appearance records are ignored, and failed cache writes can retry on the next enumeration.
- The third-party Air M1 directory contains Air 4 finishes. Air 5 now uses the original March 2022 Apple artwork, which supplies only Space Gray, rather than presenting green/silver/rose-gold as Air 5 finishes. XR white/black and 5c pink labels were also corrected.
- iPhone 18 name recognition and its four official finishes are checked explicitly; all 174 model identifiers are also exercised through the device-card display-name path.

Regression tests cover all included identifiers, every finish's embedded image/mask and color tokens, unknown/conflicting colors, four-way pointer mapping, cache restart/isolation, failed-setting rollback and preference changes without opening a preview. Physical comparison remains limited to the owner's white iPhone 12 mini and iPhone 17 color readings; synthetic and GPU checks cannot establish every real device's firmware color encoding.
